At least once a week, someone asks me, "Is everything OK back home?" or "How do you feel about what's happening in Iran?" And I usually answer with a cliché: "I hope everything turns out OK."

Sometimes I avoid revealing my nationality altogether — to dodge these questions, or worse, to spare myself the sight of people trying to act normal around me.

That's why I decided to write this short post: to express my internal chaos. Not to buy pity, not to stage sentimental theater, but to raise awareness of what is happening to people like me.

No — everything is not OK. Not OK at all. Sorry, but that's the truth. But friend, how can I explain to you that I felt happy when the war started? Would you believe me if I told you I chanted for the US to bomb the hell out of my own country? With what logic can I explain that during the World Cup, we cheered for every team in the world except Iran? How can I convince you that every night the US did not attack Iran, I went to bed disappointed and sad? And how can I make you understand that, at the very same time, I was devastated when my favorite places were bombed, when my university was bombed, when my old roommate was bombed and killed, when innocent lives were taken?

Believe me — if I could make you understand how all of these contradictory feelings live in me at once, I would. But I can't. And even if I could, I don't have the energy.

47 years ago, there was a big bang in my country. Time restarted. Everything went back. We threw away all we had and gave power to the most demonic force I have ever seen in my life, The Islamic Republic regime. I have just read what Hitler and Stalin did. But I have seen people who were tortured because of expressing their opinions, men hanged because of not believing in their faith, women raped because of not wearing a hijab, shops closed because of serving women without hijab, Bahai's and jews houses being burnt. I cannot unsee what I've seen.
